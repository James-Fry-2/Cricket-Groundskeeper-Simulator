Briefing

What it is

A management game about preparing cricket pitches. Over one season you choose which strip each match is played on, prepare it with water, rollers, mowers and covers, and live with the verdicts: the match referee's rating, the captain's requests and the board's wishes.

What we're testing

One question: is it fun to rotate strips across a season while balancing those pressures? We're not testing how it looks. It's text in a terminal on purpose, because the rules still change daily, and pictures come later if the idea works. Please judge the decisions, not the presentation.

There are no right answers, and you can't do it wrong. If something confuses you, that's useful to us.

What it takes

  • About two to three hours for a season, in one sitting or a few. It saves itself after every turn and asks to carry on next time.
  • A Mac or a Windows PC. The zip has everything, so there's nothing else to install.
  • Follow README.txt in the zip to start it, then read Guide.md, which is a few pages. The game also shows tips the first time things happen.

While you play

  • Play however you like. Some players plan everything; some take each day as it comes.
  • Notes help a lot. If anything surprises, confuses or annoys you, or a choice feels hard, jot it in notes.txt in your season's folder, however rough.
  • If the game breaks or gets stuck, note what you did just before, and send the folder anyway.

What the game records

Each season's folder holds save.json, the season so it can be replayed exactly, and telemetry.jsonl, a log of the commands you gave, the readings you took, how you answered requests, the screens you looked at, and how long each turn took. Nothing about you or your computer is recorded, and the game sends nothing anywhere.

What to send back

  1. Zip your season's folder. It's in Documents/Cricket Groundsman, named season-<date>-<number>.
  2. Answer the questionnaire below, then copy your answers or save them as a file.
  3. Send both to the person who sent you the game.

An honest "this bit dragged" is worth more to us than politeness.
